Seasonal prep guide

Every year our phones light up the morning after the first big snowfall. Buried garden edges, frozen gutters and empty salt bins cause most of those calls. The good news is that almost all of it can be avoided with an hour or two of work in October.

Why October is the month that matters

The ground is still soft enough to push in edge stakes, and gutters are full of leaves that will freeze solid by December. Contractor schedules also fill fast: most crews close their route books by mid-November, so booking early means you get the time slot you want, not what's left.

The ten-step checklist

Work through these in order. Most homes take under two hours, and the first five steps do most of the protecting.

  1. 01
    Stake lawn and garden edgesReflective stakes every 3 m show plow drivers where the grass starts.
  2. 02
    Clear gutters and downspoutsBlocked gutters freeze and turn into heavy ice dams.
  3. 03
    Mark hidden obstaclesFlag sprinkler heads, curbs, drains and low walls.
  4. 04
    Stock salt or pet-safe meltStore it dry, close to your door, before stores sell out.
  5. 05
    Book your plow routeReserve before Nov 15 to lock in early-bird pricing.
  6. 06
    Trim overhanging branchesIce-loaded limbs snap onto roofs, cars and lines.
  7. 07
    Seal driveway cracksWater in cracks freezes, expands and breaks the surface.
  8. 08
    Move vehicles and binsPick a winter spot that leaves the plow a clear path.
  9. 09
    Test outdoor lightingDark mornings plus ice is how most slips happen.
  10. 10
    Save the dispatch numberKeep 1-800-555-0199 in your phone for storm nights.

Ten minutes with a box of edge stakes saves us an hour of guessing in the dark, and saves you a torn-up lawn in spring.

Marcus Chen, Crew Lead, Northgate

Salt storage done right

Rock salt clumps the moment it gets damp. Keep it in a sealed bin with a lid, off the ground, and within reach of your front steps. If you have pets or new concrete, choose a calcium magnesium acetate blend: it costs more but won't burn paws or pit the surface.

Crew tip

Take a photo of your staked driveway and send it with your booking. Our drivers load it on their route tablets before the first storm.

When to call in the pros

If your driveway is longer than 30 m, you have a steep slope, or you leave for work before 7 AM, a seasonal plan usually costs less than a snowblower plus your time. We handle plowing, walks and ice treatment automatically once 5 cm falls, so you never have to make the call at 5 AM.
